--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY ONE REVERSAL SETTLES ALL 253
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
|Aut(Paley(23))| = 253 = C(23,2) = the number of arcs (nauty), so Aut acts
REGULARLY on arcs and in particular transitively: every single-arc reversal of
Paley(23) is isomorphic to every other.  "Reverse an arc of Paley(23)" therefore
names ONE tournament up to isomorphism, and one witness settles the family.

Verified constructively rather than from the group order alone: for every one of
the 253 arcs, the explicit affine map carrying (0,1) to that arc was checked to
carry the reversed tournament onto the reversed tournament.  0 failures.  See
WITNESSES_paley_arcrev.md.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY A PARTIAL SCAN IS A COMPLETE POSITIVE CERTIFICATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The scan was STOPPED once base state 1161 returned SAT; base states 1162..8030
were never searched.  That is sound here and would not be sound for a
refutation.  A witness exhibits the object the claim asserts exists, so the
remaining base states could only produce more witnesses.  A refutation is the
opposite: it is only as good as the union of its slices, which is why every
negative verdict in this package audits an exact index cover and every positive
one does not.

Do not apply the refutation checklist to this file.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
NO SYMMETRY BREAK WAS AVAILABLE, AND NONE WAS USED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reversing an arc destroys every automorphism: |Aut(T^e)| = 1, and the
out-degrees go from regular (11 everywhere) to {10,11,12}.  So --toppair,
--top0 and --toporb are all UNSOUND on this host and none is used; all 8,031
base states are live, against 2,591 for Paley(23) itself under its anchor.
That is the whole reason this run costs an order of magnitude more than the
34.03 core-h refutation of Paley(23).

Only the voter lex-sort applies, and it is a WLOG about voter ordering that is
independent of the host (Lemma 2.3).  This verdict therefore rests on ONE
human-checked lemma rather than two: no orbit calculation is involved.

not being 5-inducible and the whole result would be wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONCLUSION, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT GIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Paley(23) is not 5-inducible (34.03 core-h, exact coverage, and machine-checked
in p23cert_d6/).  Every single-arc reversal of it is 5-inducible.  Hence
Paley(23) is ARC-CRITICAL at plain majority.

Arc-criticality implies vertex-criticality (WITNESSES_paley_arcrev.md carries
the proof: k-inducibility is hereditary, and T^e - v = T - v when e is incident
to v), so Paley(23) is vertex-critical too.  That is corroborated independently
by the direct route: Paley(23) - v has its own witness, from base state 6560, in
n22_p23minusv.tar.zst.

At UNIT margin the picture is the opposite and is recorded elsewhere: Paley(23)
is neither vertex-critical (m1_family cell p23mv) nor arc-critical (m1_arcrev/).
The two margins come apart on this host, which is the point of the q = 23 row of
the criticality table in Section 3.2.
